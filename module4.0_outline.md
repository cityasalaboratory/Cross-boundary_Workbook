## Module summary
Cities and everyday environments are often imagined and designed as spaces made by and for humans. Yet they are continuously inhabited, shaped and transformed by plants, animals, fungi, bacteria and other non-human agents. This module introduces More-than-Human and Multispecies Design as approaches for moving beyond Human-Centred approach and reconsidering Design as a relational practice within interconnected ecological worlds. Learners will explore how anthropocentric assumptions shape urban environments and how non-human agency can be acknowledged in city design. Attention is given to living and non-living agents, whose behaviours, needs and capacities are frequently overlooked despite their central role in urban ecosystems. Through data collection, relationship mapping, reversed perspective approach and future envisioning, learners will identify non-human agents, investigate the relations and stressors that shape its everyday conditions and reflect on existing power dynamics. They will therefore transform the insights gathered into a speculative future scenario “beyond the human”, aimed at fostering a mutually beneficial coexistence between the human and non-human worlds. The module encourages participants to document a contextualised multispecies relationship, to critically question anthropocentric assumptions and to propose an possible intervention that is attentive to coexistence, care, temporality and the autonomy of different forms of life. 

## Learning Objectives

- <span style="color:#0089A6; font-weight:700;">Recognise</span> the limits of designing only for humans and why it matters to consider other living beings in the design of shared spaces.

- <span style="color:#0089A6; font-weight:700;">Learn</span> to see a place from the perspective of the plants and animals that live there.

- <span style="color:#0089A6; font-weight:700;">Imagine</span> Imagine a future that supports coexistence between humans and nature.
