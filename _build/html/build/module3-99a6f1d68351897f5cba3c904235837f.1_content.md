In the previous chapter “Connect with and define the challenge”, you considered what draws you to a shared challenge, where its boundaries lie, who are its main stakeholder, and what role you could play. The way you defined the challenge already expresses values. If you framed urban heat as a public health issue, you might ask who is most exposed and how to protect them. If you framed it as a question of access to public space, you might ask who can use parks, streets, and squares on the hottest days. Each framing directs attention to particular experiences, brings different people into the conversation, and makes certain responses easier to imagine. Setting up a collaboration gives you a chance to revisit that framing with others.

What is value-based design?

In simple words, it is a way of designing or a design approach that not only starts from, but is based on those guiding principles of our actions as individuals, citizens, or as stakeholders in the organizations that we are representing. In such an approach, we don't start with the objective, for example, reducing CO2 emissions. We look into why we are actually doing that, and that is the value of sustainability. Here, individually, we reflect on and define our values, what matters the most to us, and collectively, we try to agree on what is that thing that speaks to all of us. It could be one or more values to consider. Agreeing on what people value the most not only broadens the design opportunities, but it also leads to designing systems that are ethical and very much needed by society.
