## Setting: Case location and documentation
 La Goccia is an 18-hectare former industrial site in Milan, between the Bovisa and Villapizzone neighbourhoods. It is contaminated and has been closed for over 30 years. In that time, nature has turned it into a spontaneous urban forest with more than 15,000 trees and about 40 bird species. The GOCCIA project (Green Opportunities to Clean-up Contaminants through an Interspecies Alliance) aims to complete the clean-up using Nature-Based Solutions. It also aims to test a multispecies model that other cities can reuse.

## Context - Organisational set up and resources
The project runs from December 2024 to May 2028. It is funded by the European Urban Initiative and led by the Municipality of Milan, together with Politecnico di Milano, companies, non-profits and a local association. Three partner cities, Est Ensemble Grand Paris, Malaga and Rijeka, face similar challenges. The team must balance the urgency of cleaning the soil with the long-term care of the forest and its community.

About five hectares will be treated through bioremediation, which uses living organisms to clean the soil. An industrial building will be recovered for shared forest management. An ethnographic path, based on listening to and observing the people and places involved, will help build a participatory governance. The site will gradually open to citizens through guided visits, festivals and training.

## Results - Progress compared to the ambitions
The expected results are cleaner soil, more biodiversity and a “living lab” where people and other species learn together.

### 1 | Observe

**What issues and what opportunities can you see in La Goccia? Why does this situation matter now?**

<ol type="A">
  <li>
    <p>A contaminated site, closed for over 30 years, whose soil is a risk for people and for the life that grows on it.</p>
    <p><em>Puts the risk of contamination at the centre, for humans and non-humans alike. It may leave out that the forest is already part of the response.</em></p>
  </li>
  <li>
    <p>A spontaneous forest of over 15,000 trees and about 40 bird species, grown without human design and already helping to heal the soil.</p>
    <p><em>Recognises non-human life as an active agent, not a background. It may leave out the real health risks that people still face.</em></p>
  </li>
  <li>
    <p>A site that is reopening to the city: citizens gain a green space, but access also brings pressure on a place left undisturbed for decades.</p>
    <p><em>Highlights the encounter between human use and non-human habitat. It may leave out the contamination that makes the encounter delicate.</em></p>
  </li>
</ol>
