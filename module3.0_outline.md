## Module summary
Who gets to define a city’s challenges, whose knowledge counts, and who can influence what happens next? This module explores why human dignity matters when people learn and act together across neighborhoods, organizations, and communities. In cities where access to resources and decision-making is uneven, the way a learning pathway is designed can reinforce those inequalities or create more meaningful opportunities to participate. Designing for dignity starts from the equal worth of everyone involved and asks what conditions allow people to contribute on their own terms and shape decisions that affect them. Through the values-at-risk methodology and the dignity-based design cycle, you will examine a challenge in your city, trace how different groups experience it, and identify the values and underlying values of dignity shaping current responses. You will bring diverse perspectives into dialogue, explore where values align or come into tension, and use design for values methods to turn those insights into choices about how people learn and act together. In your Learning Journey Portfolio, you will propose a pathway for dignified learning and action. You will explain whose perspectives informed it, how your design could address barriers to participation and strengthen relationships, and what could help the value you create together endure beyond the immediate collaboration. 

## Learning Objectives

- <span style="color:#0089A6; font-weight:700;">Explain</span> why values matter in setting up cross-boundary collaboration.

- <span style="color:#0089A6; font-weight:700;">Explain</span> why dignity matters to participate, contribute, and influence decisions.

- <span style="color:#0089A6; font-weight:700;">Use</span> design-for-values approaches to analyse where perspectives align or come into tension.

- <span style="color:#0089A6; font-weight:700;">Design</span> a pathway for dignified learning that strengtens mutual relationships beyond the initial collaboration.
